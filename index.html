Rently: Fixing Rental Transparency in BC
UX Research & Design · Case Study
Rently

Overview

TypeCommunity Rental Platform
LocationMetro Vancouver, BC

Rently is a community rental platform where renters share verified, anonymous reviews of buildings and landlords, including the true monthly cost. It gives renters across Metro Vancouver the information listings leave out, so they can sign a lease with confidence.

RoleUX/UI Designer, Wireframes & Presentation
TeamPolina, Bethia, Ayan (group project)
ToolsFigma
MethodsSurvey · Competitive Analysis

The Problem

Residents in British Columbia need a reliable way to understand what renting a specific home is really like: true monthly cost, landlord behaviour, and building condition. Current rental listings lack that transparency, and the gap leads to stressful, sometimes financially harmful rental experiences that better information could prevent.

User Context

Canada is in a major housing crisis. Metro Vancouver's rental vacancy rate has ticked up to 3.7% (CMHC, 2025), but costs remain extremely high for students and young adults. Popular listing sites are built for landlords, which leaves renters short on the information that matters most.

  • Hidden expenses: advertised prices rarely include utilities.
  • Misleading photos & maintenance: listing photos hide real issues.
  • One-sided background checks: landlords screen tenants, but tenants get no visibility into landlord responsiveness.
The Opportunity

Rently is a community-driven platform built to fix this: by letting renters share verified, honest reviews, Rently brings transparency to the rental market so people can make housing decisions with confidence.

Research

How We Researched It

We ran a short survey with 5 people across BC, 4 of whom currently rent, mixing open and closed questions about rent history, hidden fees, trust, and what a transparency tool should look like. To widen the picture, we pulled real renter complaints from r/canadahousing, where people across the country talk openly about what went wrong. Small sample, but the patterns lined up closely with what we saw online.

Persona

SL
Sarah Lee
24 · Burnaby, BC · Recent Grad

A recent grad working full time, she just moved out of her family home to find her first apartment in Metro Vancouver. With the market this competitive, she wants to research every option before she commits, but the details she cares about most are scattered across Reddit and Facebook groups.

"Most listings leave out the details that actually matter: building issues, landlord reliability, hidden fees. I check Reddit and social media, but it's scattered across a dozen places instead of being in one."

  • Know what a place is really like before moving in.
  • Avoid unreliable landlords and surprise costs.
  • Compare options using trustworthy info.
  • Feel confident before signing a lease.
Needs
  • Verified renter experiences.
  • Reliable landlord info.
  • Building condition insights.
  • Rental history info.
  • A safe platform to share and access reviews.

Visual Identity

A warm, grounded palette inspired by Vancouver streets and old brick buildings, built to make renters feel safe and informed.

Built for repeat visits: 3 of 5 renters said they'd check Rently at every stage, not just before signing.

Testing

Usability Testing

We put the prototype in front of renters and asked them to find a building, check its true monthly cost, and submit a rent report. Testers said the design felt polished enough to trust, which mattered for an app built on credibility. The listing cards and map filtering by rating and rent were the standouts. The noise meter was the weak spot: people couldn't tell what the segments meant. That's the first thing we'd redesign, likely with labels or a short scale explanation.

What Worked
  • Polished design built trust
  • Listing cards on the home page
  • Map filtering by rating and rent
What to Fix
  • Noise meter segments were unclear

What I Learned

The most important design decision in Rently was making anonymity feel trustworthy rather than suspicious. Users were willing to share sensitive information about their landlords and rental costs, but only when the interface communicated clearly that their identity was protected. Privacy UI is just as important as the data architecture beneath it.

Sonder Coffee & Bar

Overview

VerticalHospitality · Local Business

Sonder is a neighbourhood spot that’s a coffee shop by day and a cocktail bar by night. I built the brand from the ground up, including the identity, menus, website, event posters and social content, all designed to make one space feel like two different experiences without losing its warm, moody character.

sonder.com

Style Guide

My Role and Tools

RoleUX/UI Designer
ToolsFigma · Canva
DeliverablesStrategy · UGC · Copywriting · Ad Creative

The Problem

Sonder is two businesses in one room: a quiet coffee shop in the morning and a cocktail bar at night. Most cafés that add a bar end up with a split personality online, so daytime regulars never hear about the evenings and night guests never think of it for coffee. Sonder needed one brand and one social presence that sells both sides without confusing either crowd.

Process

Strategy

Content should feel like an invitation into a familiar spot, not an ad for a business. The strategy leans into Sonder's dual identity: the same space, completely different energy after 6.

01
Daytime Ritual
Coffee, quiet corners, regulars. The comfort of a familiar order.
02
Evening Transformation
Lighting shift, drink menu, music. The same space, completely different energy.
03
Behind the Counter
Baristas, bartenders, small moments. The human texture behind the brand.
UGC Concept

A short filmed piece following one visit from a first coffee order in the morning through returning that same evening for a drink, showing the space's transformation. Framed as "a day at Sonder," not a product ad.

Copywriting
"Your 8am order knows you better than most people do."
"Same seat. Same corner. Completely different energy after 6."
"Not everything needs a name on the menu. Ask what's good tonight."
Ad Creative

Banner set built for MDIA4016, tying the warm terracotta and navy brand palette to both the daytime and nighttime identity. Designed to run as a matched pair across a full day-part rotation.

Results and Reflection

What I Learned

The dual identity of Sonder (coffee shop by day, bar by night) became the entire creative strategy. When a brand already has a natural narrative arc, the best content just follows it.

Dripped is a location based fashion discovery app where users browse outfits posted nearby, upload their own looks and tap individual pieces to find them locally. It blends social media, style inspiration and shopping into one feed, with a bold chrome visual identity built to stand out to a style forward Gen Z audience.

Style Guide

My Role and Tools

RoleUX/UI Designer
ToolsFigma · Canva

The Problem

Gen Z sees outfits they love online and on the street, screenshots them, and never finds the pieces again. Fashion apps are built around big brands and online checkout, not the style people see around them every day, so there is no easy way to find where a look came from or buy it locally.

Process

Strategy

02

Personal style, individuality. Your aesthetic, mapped.
03

Gen Z doesn't respond to ads that look like ads. The most effective creative for Dripped is content that looks like something a real user made: native, fast, and rooted in a genuine discovery moment.

SOLENA: A Coastal Spa in Tofino
Branding · Web Design · Wellness · Case Study
SOLENA

Overview

VerticalWellness · Hospitality
LocationTofino, BC
TypeConcept Work

SOLENA is a coastal wellness spa in Tofino, offering sauna and hydrotherapy, massage, facials and body treatments inspired by the West Coast. I created the brand identity and a fully clickable website, from the service menu and booking flow to a take home product shop with a working cart and checkout. The visual direction is calm, earthy and natural, designed to feel like the rainforest and ocean that surround it.

Style Guide

My Role and Tools

RoleBrand & Web Designer
ToolsFigma · Anima · Canva · Netlify
DeliverablesBrand Identity · Website · Shop & Checkout · Menu · Social · Reel

The Experience

01Sauna & Hydrotherapyfrom $85
02Massagefrom $140
03Facialsfrom $95
04Body Treatmentsfrom $175
05Take Home Product Linefrom $18

The Problem

Most spas sell an escape but look like a clinic: white walls, stock photos and a service menu that's hard to make sense of. In Tofino, spas compete on the view, but few build a brand around it. SOLENA needed to feel like the place itself: the forest, the cold water and the quiet.

Process

The Insight

The setting is the product. Everything is built around one idea: a spa built into the forest, not the other way around. The copy, photography and layout all point back to the outdoors instead of the treatment room.

Visual Direction

Where most spa brands lean bright and clinical, SOLENA uses the colours of the coast: sage green, deep forest olive, warm cream and soft tan, with an elegant serif and lots of space. Dark sections feel like the rainforest at dusk, and cream sections feel like a quiet morning.

The Website

I designed the full site in Figma for desktop and mobile, then turned it into a responsive build and deployed it on Netlify so people can actually use it. Visitors can read the menu, book a time, and shop the take home line with a working cart and checkout.

Results and Reflection

Why It Works

SOLENA doesn't sell a list of treatments. It sells a feeling of being somewhere, and every touchpoint from the menu to the checkout keeps that feeling going.

What I Learned

Taking SOLENA from Figma to a live site showed me how much lives between the design and the build. Exact colour values, how text sits on each background and how a page behaves on different devices all decide whether it still feels premium once it's real.

Oneway: Travel App for Real Travellers
Travel · App Design · UX/UI · Case Study
oneway

Overview

TypeTravel App · Web & Mobile
AudienceSolo travellers · Community seekers

Oneway is a travel platform for people who want to explore the world and meet others doing the same. Users can discover destinations, book trips, see fellow travellers on a live map, join local meetups and connect with people along the way, all on the web and in a companion mobile app.

Artifacts

oneway.com
Open full site ↗

Style Guide

My Role and Tools

RoleUX/UI Designer
ToolsFigma · Canva
DeliverablesApp Design · Web · Brand · Strategy

The Problem

It solves two problems that every solo traveller knows: not knowing if a place is actually worth visiting right now, and not knowing anyone when you get there.

Process

Marketing Angle

Oneway's marketing is built around one insight: the best part of travel isn't the destination, it's the people you meet and the moments you didn't plan. Every piece of content leads with community and real experience.

01
Real Right Now
UGC-style content showing real spots through the live map. No staged photography.
02
Find Your People
Community stories: people who met through Oneway and ended up travelling together.
03
Go Somewhere New
Destination content driven by the live map, showing places worth visiting right now.
Copywriting
"Travel further. Arrive somewhere."
"See it before you go. Know before you arrive."
"Solo travel. Shared experience."
Key Features
Live Map: Is It Worth It Right Now?
An interactive map showing real-time photos posted by travellers at tourist spots. Posts expire after 24 hours, so you always see what a place actually looks like today. Each spot shows crowd-sourced yes/no answers: Is there clean drinking water? Are there washrooms? Real information, no wasted trips.
Swipe to Connect: Find Your People
A swipe-based matching feature for travellers in the same location. Swipe right on someone whose travel style matches yours and connect for a day trip, a meal, or just a coffee. Built like a dating app in its simplicity, but for friendship and community on the road.
Meetup Posts: Community Events
Travellers can post meetups: a hike, a beach day, a night out. Anyone in the area can see and join. Turns solo travel into a shared experience without the awkwardness of cold messaging strangers.
Booking: All in One Place
Flights, accommodation, and activities bookable within the app. Oneway is community first, booking second, but having it all in one place means you never have to leave the app.

Results and Reflection

What I Learned

The most interesting design challenge in Oneway is the tension between community and privacy: people want to connect with strangers while travelling, but they also don't want to feel unsafe. Every design decision around the swipe feature and meetup posts had to balance openness with enough friction to keep it feeling like a travel community, not something else.
